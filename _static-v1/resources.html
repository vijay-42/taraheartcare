What to Expect

Your First Visit

Coming to see a cardiologist for the first time? Here's how your visit will go.

Book & Arrive Early

Book online or by phone. Please arrive 15 minutes early for registration.

Bring Your Records

Previous ECGs, echo / angiography reports, blood tests, discharge summaries and a list of current medicines.

Consultation

The cardiologist reviews your history and symptoms, examines you, and checks your BP and ECG.

Tests (if needed)

Any required tests are usually done the same day on-site.

Treatment Plan

You leave with a clear diagnosis, a prescription, and a follow-up date.

Pre-Test Instructions

Preparing for Your Test

Following these simple steps helps us get accurate results.

  • Fast for 10–12 hours before the test. Plain water is allowed.
  • Avoid alcohol and fatty food the evening before.
  • Take your regular BP / heart medicines unless told otherwise.
  • Diabetic patients: ask us about insulin / tablet timing.
  • Wear comfortable clothes and sports shoes / walking shoes.
  • Have a light meal 2–3 hours before. Do not come on a full stomach.
  • Avoid tea, coffee and smoking for 3–4 hours before the test.
  • Some medicines (e.g. beta-blockers) may need to be stopped. Ask your doctor first.
  • Men may need the chest area shaved for electrode placement.
  • No fasting or special preparation needed.
  • Wear a two-piece outfit (top and bottom) for easy access to the chest.
  • Avoid applying body lotion or oil on the chest on the day of the test.
  • Bathe before coming. The device must not get wet while worn.
  • Wear loose clothing that fits over the device.
  • Keep a simple diary of activities and symptoms during the recording.
  • Return the device at the scheduled time the next day.
  • Fast for 6 hours before the procedure.
  • Inform us about allergies (especially to iodine / contrast dye) and kidney problems.
  • Blood thinners and diabetes medicines (e.g. metformin) may need adjustment. Follow your doctor's advice.
  • Bring all previous reports and come with one family member.

Recognise the Early Signs of a Heart Attack

  • Chest pain, pressure, tightness or heaviness
  • Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back
  • Sudden breathlessness
  • Cold sweat, nausea or vomiting
  • Unusual tiredness or dizziness
  • "Gas" or acidity-like discomfort that doesn't settle

Don't wait for symptoms to pass. Call +91 00000 00000 immediately. Women, diabetics and the elderly may have milder or unusual symptoms.
