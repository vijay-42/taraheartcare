Interventional Cardiology (Cath Lab)

Minimally invasive, image-guided procedures to diagnose and open blocked heart arteries, available round the clock for emergencies.

Coronary Angiography (Radial / Femoral)

Coronary angiography is the gold-standard test to see blockages in the heart's arteries. A thin, flexible tube (catheter) is passed through an artery in the wrist (radial) or groin (femoral) to the heart, and a contrast dye shows the arteries on X-ray.

  • Done under local anaesthesia; you stay awake and comfortable
  • Radial (wrist) approach: walk within hours, less bleeding
  • Results discussed with you and your family right after the procedure
20–30 min procedureOften same-day discharge

Angioplasty & Stenting (PTCA)

If angiography shows a significant blockage, it can often be treated without open-heart surgery. A tiny balloon opens the narrowed artery, and a drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube) is placed to keep it open and restore blood flow.

  • Latest-generation drug-eluting stents
  • Intravascular imaging (IVUS / OCT) for complex cases [if available]
  • Most patients return to normal activity within a week
1–2 day stayCashless insurance

Primary Angioplasty: Emergency Heart Attack Care

During a heart attack, a coronary artery is suddenly blocked and heart muscle starts dying within minutes. Primary angioplasty opens the blocked artery immediately in the Cath Lab. It is the most effective treatment for a major heart attack and saves both life and heart muscle.

  • 24 × 7 × 365 Cath Lab team on call
  • Pre-alert system: call ahead so the team is ready when you arrive
  • Target "door-to-balloon" time under 90 minutes

Pacemaker & Device Implantation

When the heart beats too slowly or irregularly, a small battery-powered device placed under the skin keeps the rhythm steady. We also implant advanced devices for heart failure and life-threatening rhythm problems.

  • Single & dual-chamber pacemakers (MRI-compatible options)
  • ICD: protection against sudden cardiac arrest
  • CRT: improves pumping in advanced heart failure
  • Device follow-up and programming clinic
Local anaesthesia2–3 day stay
